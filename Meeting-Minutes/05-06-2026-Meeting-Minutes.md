# Meeting #4 — IDSA Task Force on Data Spaces and AI

**Date:** 5 June 2026 · **Time:** 11:00 – 11:45 CEST · **Format:** Microsoft Teams (recorded)

---

## Meeting highlights

**Position paper status.** Content must be finalized by 15 June. Achim Meyer will add concrete content to several subsections. Viivi Lähteenoja has already contributed to Chapter 2 and the ethical and societal context. One meeting remains before finalization.

**Lead with the opportunity, not the problems.** Reinhold Achatz argued the draft over-emphasizes problems and underplays what data spaces offer AI: governance, traceability and compensation for data owners. The first paper should lead with solutions and lean positive. Mohammad Yasser agreed and proposed naming each subsection after an AI problem that data spaces solve, with trust moved to the front.

**Agreed structure.** The chapter on how data spaces solve AI challenges becomes the largest part, framed positively. A following chapter covers how AI adds value to data spaces, possibly absorbing Olaf Gerd Gemein's Knowledge Graph and Knowledge Core material. New agent-related challenges go in a smaller section near the end. Roland Fadrany advised keeping challenges before solutions. Reinhold proposed combining Chapters 3 and 4 in a challenge-to-solution format. Chapters 1 and 2 keep their position.

**International perspective.** The introduction should open with an international view, acknowledging a European flavor on sovereignty and privacy. The regulatory context should include non-EU approaches. Masaru Dobashi shared candidate text on Japan's soft-law approach. The paper stays short and crisp for a broad audience.

**Cross-references.** The paper will cross-reference the Fraunhofer and Fujitsu paper and the forthcoming training-data bottleneck paper. The naming "Data Spaces and AI" is used consistently.

**Future roadmap.** Haluk Inanmis proposed a forward-looking section on autonomous agents, noting open questions around agent identity, liability and direct agentic access. Roland noted liability stays with an organization and that data spaces need criteria for which agents may process which data, a direction Gaia-X is examining. This can fit into the Dataspace Protocol and Model Context Protocol discussion.

---

## Decisions

- Chapter 4 reframed around the value data spaces offer AI, framed positively, as the largest part of the paper. Trust moves to the front.
- A dedicated chapter on how AI adds value to data spaces, potentially with the Knowledge Graph and Knowledge Core material 
- New agent challenges placed in a smaller section near the end.
- Challenges kept before solutions. Chapters 3 and 4 may be combined.
- Introduction opens with an international perspective; regulatory context includes non-EU examples.
- Cross-references to the Fraunhofer and Fujitsu paper and the forthcoming training-data paper from the Telco community.
- Naming "Data Spaces and AI" used consistently.
