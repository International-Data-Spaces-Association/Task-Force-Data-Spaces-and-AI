# Meeting #6: IDSA Task Force on Data Spaces and AI

**Date:** September 25, 2026 · **Time:** 11:20 – 12:20 CEST · **Format:** Microsoft Teams (recorded)

---

## Meeting highlights

**Working mode.** The Task Force resumes after the summer break with bi-weekly meetings. Sessions are recorded and minutes are published, so full attendance is welcome but not required.

**Feedback on the position paper.** Lars Nagel described the paper as a complete stocktaking of how data spaces and AI tie together and asked the group to keep that positioning: IDSA shows how activities in the field relate to data spaces and is not a research organization. The open questions at the end of the paper can be used to track progress, for example every quarter. A network of testbeds fits IDSA's international role and could connect existing efforts such as those at the University of Tokyo and in Manufacturing-X.

**Testbed interest in Japan.** Akira Sakaino shared the paper with Japanese industry and government contacts, many of whom want to join a testbed. NTT has decided to develop a new solution for AI, data spaces and trust. After Data Space Discovery Day Tokyo, the Data Society Alliance (DSA) asked to join a testbed and offered a data quality notary feature. Akira proposed starting the activity under IDSA together with the University of Tokyo.

**Agent governance builds on data space governance.** Christoph Schlueter Langdon reported on joint work with NTT on federated identity and trust anchor solutions. What defines an agent is agency, a principal relationship, which raises the same trust and control questions data spaces already address at the network layer. Deutsche Telekom is developing Agentic Hub capabilities that extend sovereign control to the agent level and offered them as a contribution.

**Board input on next outputs.** Members added their priorities to the Miro board. Achim Meyer proposed guidance for developers on why the data space approach, based on World Wide Web Consortium (W3C) standards, is the right choice for inter-organizational agents. João Ronaldo Del Ducca Cunha (Eldorado, Brazil) proposed sharing computational resources such as supercomputers via data spaces. Roland Fadrany reported that Gaia-X is building a regulation mapping tool, planned for the Gaia-X Summit in November, that turns regulations into executable criteria. Christoph asked for draft standards on agent governance and a use case package with a business value calculation, a software demo and a paper.

**Scoping the testbed.** Achim asked what the testbed is meant to be: a place for agents to test their data space abilities, an AI compliance testbed or an open sandbox. Reinhold Achatz proposed a proof of concept in which AI agents work together with classical participants, defining the environment first and the use cases next or in parallel. Yasser Mohammad noted that visions differ and need to be unified; for him a testbed is at least one worked use case plus a sandbox where others can add new ones. Both directions should be considered, including whether data spaces face new requirements to become AI-ready.

**Audience and positioning.** Johannes Schramm argued that the wider audience cares most about how AI benefits from data spaces, so the entry barrier for newcomers should stay low. Sohvi Virkkula stressed positioning data spaces now within the EU AI Factories, Data Labs and Testing and Experimentation Facilities (TEFs). Olaf Gerd Gemein noted that the momentum of AI far exceeds that of data spaces, so data space governance should be positioned as the natural governance structure for AI and agentic frameworks.

**Needs and speed.** Akira proposed a survey of IDSA members and other stakeholders on concerns, risks and expectations, followed by a simple AI-enabled data space for IDSA documents as a demonstrator. Yasser supported the survey and having at least one demo or testbed as an output. Christoph cautioned that IDSA is following rather than leading on this topic, so work must run in parallel to stay relevant. Achim suggested also approaching teams that already run cross-company agent projects to learn their pain points. Reinhold confirmed broad agreement: a limited number of activities, run in parallel.

---

## Decisions

- Bi-weekly meetings continue; sessions are recorded and minutes published in this repository.
- A limited number of activities run in parallel; the survey does not hold up other work.
- An informal testbed group (not an official IDSA working group) brings interested members and initiatives together. Its first task is to define the purpose and environment of the testbed.
- The Miro board entries will be turned into concrete work items with clear outcomes and success criteria, forming the next version of the backlog.
- Special interest groups form around items with enough contributors, possibly including external ones.
