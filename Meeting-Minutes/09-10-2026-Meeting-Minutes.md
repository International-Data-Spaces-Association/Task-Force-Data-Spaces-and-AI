# Meeting #7: International Data Spaces Association (IDSA) Task Force on Data Spaces and AI

**Date:** October 9, 2026 · **Time:** 11:03 – 11:33 CEST · **Format:** Microsoft Teams (recorded)

---

## Meeting highlights

**Member survey.** The survey is ready but not yet sent. Akira Sakaino tested it and found no issues. Feedback is welcome until Tuesday, October 13; distribution follows that week.

**Four pillars.** The Miro entries are now grouped into four pillars: agentic governance and trust; AI-ready technology and architecture; use cases, value and outreach; and testbed use cases and demonstrations. The testbed runs in parallel as a separate interest group, with interested parties from Japan and Europe.

**More substance per item.** Olaf Gerd Gemein noted that the entries are still headlines. He proposed short one-pagers with objectives and purpose, an architecture framework view and a look at existing work (e.g., public International Telecommunication Union (ITU) contributions) to avoid reinventing the wheel. He suggested a structured questionnaire to collect member input, using AI agents to draft it and analyze the answers.

**Link to the DSSC Blueprint.** Haluk Inanmis proposed reviewing the Data Spaces Support Centre (DSSC) Blueprint building blocks for the impact of agentic AI, since a recently published data space maturity standard uses the same blocks. He noted that legal and organizational aspects are missing (e.g., EU AI Act, diverging regional rules for agents acting across data spaces) and that use cases should follow business needs. Updates to the DSSC technical building blocks are expected ahead of the Data Spaces Symposium in March.

**Common basis.** Achim Meyer pointed to a gap between the position paper, which covers all data space concepts, and recent IDSA working group discussions, which assume the Dataspace Protocol (DSP) and the Decentralized Claims Protocol (DCP). He asked the group to agree on its basis in order to offer concrete value rather than an overview. Olaf cautioned against imposing one approach and suggested framing IDSA's role as showing how data spaces contribute to trustworthy AI. Both agreed to focus where the case is strongest while staying useful to other concepts.

**Funding opportunity.** Anil Turkmayali shared a Eureka Network (Globalstars) call relevant to later testbed phases. The deadline is January 20, 2027 and a Japanese partner is required. Funding follows national rules (for German small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) up to 60%, via the national funding agency). IDSA's possible role in a consortium is still open.

**New participants.** Elsa Winterhalter joined on behalf of Mobility Data Space and asked for access to the Miro board and the survey.

---

## Decisions

- The survey goes out in the week of October 12, after member feedback closes on October 13.
- The backlog stays organized in four pillars; the testbed continues in parallel as an interest group.
- Each pillar gets a fuller description (tasks, definition of done) and members add input through a structured questionnaire.
- Legal and organizational aspects will be covered, either within the governance pillar or as a fifth pillar.
- The group's common basis (DSP and DCP focus vs. broader data space concepts) stays open and will be revisited.
